# ABS Personas derived from ACRP 25

| Persona ID | Name | Key traits (for ABS logic) | Typical processes used | Example sources you can cite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Domestic O&D – Business | Arrives relatively late (closer to departure), light baggage (often cabin only), familiar with airport, prefers fast/short queues, often uses priority check‑in/security if available. | Check‑in (desk or kiosk), baggage drop (sometimes none), security, boarding; rarely uses meeters/greeters. | ACRP 25 Vol 1 – terminal users and planning process (Ch. II, IV); airport service‑level docs mentioning business vs leisure segmentation.[onlinepubs.trb+1](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/acrp/acrp_rpt_025v1.pdf) |
| P2 | Domestic O&D – Leisure / Family | Arrives early, more bags, may travel in groups, slower at processing steps, more likely to dwell in retail/F&B, more sensitive to wayfinding and queue stress. | Check‑in, baggage drop, security, may spend longer in retail/food, boarding; often uses meeters/greeters on arrivals. | ACRP 25 Vol 1 – discussion of terminal users, meeters/greeters, and ground access; ACI/IATA SLA docs mentioning leisure segment and discretionary dwell.[onlinepubs.trb+1](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/acrp/acrp_rpt_025v1.pdf) |
| P3 | International O&D – Departing | Arrives very early (longer check‑in and immigration buffer), usually checked baggage, must pass outbound passport control, may visit duty‑free and lounges. | Check‑in, baggage drop, outbound passport control/immigration, security (if not combined), duty‑free/retail, boarding. | ACRP 25 Vol 1 – sections on international facilities and passenger flows (departing/arriving, domestic vs international); IATA/ACI SLA passenger segmentation by departure/arrival and business/leisure.[onlinepubs.trb+1](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/acrp/acrp_rpt_025v1.pdf) |
| P4 | International O&D – Arriving | Goes through inbound passport control, baggage claim, and customs; may have longer processing times; often met by greeters or continues to ground transport. | Inbound passport control, baggage claim, customs, meeters/greeters, ground transport. | ACRP 25 Vol 1 – international arrivals and FIS/CBP‑related guidance; ACRP 25 Vol 2 FIS model for flows (as supporting reference).[onlinepubs.trb+1](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/acrp/acrp_rpt_025v1.pdf) |
| P5 | Domestic Transfer / Connecting | Usually stays airside, often does **not** use landside check‑in or baggage claim, may need security only if re‑screened, focused on making tight connections, shorter dwell. | Deplaning, possibly security (if re‑screen), walking between gates, boarding next flight; rarely interacts with landside facilities. | ACRP 25 Vol 1 & Vol 2 – clear distinction between O&D and connecting passengers and note that connecting mostly remain airside.[onlinepubs.trb+1](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/acrp/acrp_rpt_025v1.pdf) |
| P6 | International Transfer / Connecting | May require security and/or transit immigration depending on airport layout, often time‑sensitive, can have higher stress; may use lounges if premium. | Deplaning, transit security and/or immigration, waiting in holdroom or lounges, boarding next flight; rarely uses check‑in/bag claim. | ACRP 25 Vol 1 – international terminal planning and flows; ACRP 25 Vol 2 – models for FIS and security, which distinguish connecting vs O&D.[onlinepubs.trb+1](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/acrp/acrp_rpt_025v1.pdf) |
| P7 | Premium (any market) | Higher willingness to pay, expects shorter queues and better comfort, often uses dedicated check‑in, fast track security, and lounges; arrival pattern similar to business O&D. | Priority check‑in, priority bag drop, fast‑track security, lounges, priority boarding. | IATA/ACI service level agreement guidelines describing “premium passengers” and premium lounges; ACI SLA framework discussing segmentation (premium vs regular).[aci](https://aci.aero/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Airport-Service-Level-Agreement-Guidelines.pdf) |
